LESALE-METSI — Where Every Mind Can Take Shape

"Lore lo ojwa le sale metsi."

An object is shaped while it is still flexible.

Neurodivergent Learners Adaptive Technology Inclusive Education

Lesale-Metsi is an inclusive educational technology initiative built for neurodivergent children and young learners — including those with autism, ADHD, dyslexia, and other differences in how they process information, communicate, focus, and learn. It's built on a simple belief: children don't all learn the same way, and education shouldn't expect them to. Instead of asking a child to adapt to a rigid classroom, Lesale-Metsi uses technology to adapt the learning experience to the child — through visual, audio, interactive and text-based content, so each learner can engage in whatever way works best for them.

This project started in 2024 and is still being built, because it started with a real concern for my nephew. He has ADHD, doesn't talk much, and can be hyperactive — yet he navigates phones and tablets with total ease, no help needed. Watching that made me ask something bigger: is that technology actually safe for him, in his condition? Does it adjust to how he processes the world, or is he just adapting to it because he has no other choice? Lesale-Metsi exists to flip that question — technology should adapt to the child, not the other way around.

Mark — the inspiration behind Lesale-Metsi

The Problem

Many traditional learning environments are built around an assumed "standard" learner. That can quietly shut out children who experience:

  • Difficulty processing large amounts of information at once
  • Different attention and concentration patterns
  • Challenges with reading or written communication
  • Sensory sensitivities
  • Difficulty transitioning between activities
  • Different communication needs
  • Anxiety around unfamiliar learning environments
  • Difficulty expressing when they need help

None of that means a child lacks intelligence or potential — the environment may simply not be designed for the way they learn.

The Lesale-Metsi Approach

Instead of asking — "How do we make the child fit the classroom?"

Lesale-Metsi asks — "How can we make the learning environment more responsive to the child?"

Every learner gets an individual profile, so their experience can adjust to their needs and preferences:

Visual learner→Visual instructions & diagrams
Audio preference→Text-to-speech & spoken instructions
Reading difficulty→Simplified layouts & assisted reading
Attention challenges→Shorter activities & structured steps
Sensory sensitivity→Reduced visual stimulation
Communication difficulty→Alternative communication tools
Learner Teacher Parent Therapist Community Technology

Why the Name Matters

"Lore lo ojwa le sale metsi" doesn't suggest that neurodivergent children need to be fixed or reshaped. Its deeper meaning is that early support creates room for potential to develop. That's why Lesale-Metsi focuses on early identification of learning needs, early support, personalised learning, and an environment where different minds can genuinely thrive.

Vision

A future where every child can learn in a way that respects how their mind works.

Mission

To use accessible, adaptive technology to build inclusive learning environments where neurodivergent children can learn, communicate, participate, and reach their potential.

Long-Term Vision

Long term, Lesale-Metsi could grow beyond a single app into an African neurodiversity and inclusive-learning ecosystem — with tools for schools, teachers, parents, learners, and special-needs educators, educational psychologists and support professionals. Just as importantly, it should be built with neurodivergent learners and their families, not just for them.
